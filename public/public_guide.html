1. What is DietShift Atlas? (In Plain English)

Have you ever stood in a grocery store aisle wondering why an ultra-processed snack packed with additives is cheaper than a punnet of fresh strawberries? Or whether a package stamped "Rich in Protein" or "100% Plant-Based" is genuinely wholesome or just clever marketing?

DietShift Atlas is a public, open-access digital almanac that lets you explore these questions using hard, transparent data. Instead of relying on corporate marketing claims or generic opinions, it unifies 8 major European scientific and statistical databases:

  • EFSA FoodEx2: The official European dietary surveys tracking what families across Europe actually eat.
  • ADEME Agribalyse 3.1: Detailed environmental audits measuring greenhouse gases, water usage, and land footprint for 2,451 foods.
  • Open Food Facts: Real barcode nutritional labels from grocery stores across the globe.
  • EUIPO & Qualigeo: The complete register of 6,029 European protected agricultural traditions (DOP, IGP, DOC).
  • National Statistical Institutes: Official household food spending from ISTAT (Italy), INE (Spain), INSEE (France), CBS (Netherlands), Destatis (Germany), and ONS (UK).
  • GS1 Italy Immagino & NielsenIQ: Scanner data on 151,203 products and 84,817 digitized nutritional panels across €57.3 Billion in supermarket sales.

2. The Core Mystery: The "Intention-to-Plate" Paradox

Surveys show that 72% of European citizens actively want to buy healthier, more sustainable groceries. Yet, when we examine cash-register scanner receipts, healthy and sustainable items represent less than 28% of grocery baskets.

The Attrition Waterfall
DietShift Atlas maps this 82% drop-off across a 5-stage behavioral cascade:
[Stage 1: Cultural Awareness] 100% of citizens hear about health & climate in news and media │ ▼ (Drop -28%: Information overload, conflicting diets & claims) [Stage 2: Stated Intention] 72% actively want to buy cleaner, less-processed groceries │ ▼ (Drop -31%: The "Price Wall", inflation, habitual convenience) [Stage 3: Retail Checkout Basket] 41% of basket contents survive the shelf algorithm & promotions │ ▼ (Drop -13%: Household spoilage, expiration & food waste) [Stage 4: True Dietary Ingestion] 28% of meals meet health guidelines │ ▼ (Drop -10%: Cooking emissions & supply-chain leakage) [Stage 5: Net Planetary Benefit] 18% actual carbon and water savings realized

3. The 4 Layers of the System (How It Works)

Following the multi-agent pedagogical framework of Egonex-AI/Understand-Anything, the system is designed in 4 clear, transparent layers:

  1. Tier 1 — Data Ingestion & Live Scrapling: Collects raw food facts, EAN barcodes, and protected origins using Python and D4Vinci Scrapling to keep data fresh without getting blocked.
  2. Tier 2 — In-Browser Columnar Storage (DuckDB-Wasm): Downloaded directly into your web browser. No cloud server tracking your clicks. You can query 11 Parquet tables in milliseconds right on your laptop!
  3. Tier 3 — AI & Econometric Forecasting: Uses Google TimesFM 2.0 (a cutting-edge foundation model) to forecast price and consumption trends up to 2028, and calculates Push vs. Pull market elasticity.
  4. Tier 4 — Reactive Almanac UI: Clean, publication-grade screens styled in our restrained Hallmark design system.

5. Key Concepts Demystified

What is a "Metaproduct"?

Imagine putting every single food product in a supermarket into one giant blender—weighted by how many units people actually buy. The resulting nutrition per 100g reveals the nation's true dietary baseline. In Italy, the GS1 Metaproduct averages 173.9 kcal, 6.6g protein, 7.6g sugar, and 2.9g saturated fat.

What does "Push vs. Pull" mean?

Push is when manufacturers flood supermarket shelves with new varieties (e.g. dozens of high-protein yogurts and bars). Pull is when consumers demand something organically (e.g. surging demand for chia seeds or zero-pesticide-residue produce).

What is NOVA Classification?

Developed by the University of São Paulo, NOVA groups food by how much it was industrially processed:

  • NOVA 1: Whole or minimally processed (fresh fruit, raw grains, pasteurized milk, eggs).
  • NOVA 2: Culinary ingredients (olive oil, butter, salt, sugar).
  • NOVA 3: Traditional processed food (fresh bakery bread, canned tuna in oil, aged cheese).
  • NOVA 4 (Ultra-Processed): Industrial formulations with emulsifiers, colors, and synthetic flavorings.

6. Built for Public Transparency

Unlike most modern web dashboards that track your clicks and store your data on private corporate servers, DietShift Atlas runs 100% locally in your browser using WebAssembly (DuckDB-Wasm). All databases and code blueprints are public, open-source, and verifiable.
